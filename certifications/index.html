Global Information Assurance Certification (GIAC) · SANS SEC588
GCPN — GIAC Cloud Penetration Tester
Moses Frost, Aaron Cure
GCPN validates the ability to conduct adversarial assessments against cloud-native infrastructure. SEC588 goes beyond checkbox compliance — covering IAM privilege escalation, cross-account pivoting, serverless exploitation, container escapes, and CI/CD pipeline abuse across AWS, Azure, and GCP. The course, led by Moses Frost and Aaron Cure, takes a threat-actor perspective throughout: environment mapping and service discovery, cloud-native attack paths, and targeting the abstractions that organisations rely on but rarely harden.

Offensive Security
OSCP — Offensive Security Certified Professional
The OSCP is the industry benchmark for hands-on offensive security. Candidates are dropped into an isolated network and have 24 hours to compromise a set of machines through any means necessary — no hints, no walkthroughs. Passing requires demonstrating persistence, methodical enumeration, and the ability to chain primitives under time pressure. An OSCP has proven they can think offensively in conditions that mirror real engagements.

Zero Day Engineering
Hypervisor Vulnerability Research
Alissa Esage
Focused on finding and exploiting vulnerabilities in hypervisors and virtualisation software — one of the narrowest and most technically demanding attack surfaces in offensive security. Taught by Alissa Esage, whose research has produced multiple VM-escape and hypervisor CVEs. The course covers hypervisor internals, attack surface enumeration, and hands-on exploitation of virtualisation vulnerabilities.
trainsec
Windows Internals
Pavel Yosifovich
A deep technical course on the Windows kernel — object model, process and thread management, virtual memory internals, I/O subsystem, and synchronisation primitives. Taught by Pavel Yosifovich, co-author of the definitive Windows Internals books. This is foundational knowledge for anyone working at the exploit development or kernel vulnerability research level: understanding what the OS is doing underneath gives you a precise map of where it can be made to do something else.
